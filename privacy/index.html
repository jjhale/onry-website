Onry

Privacy Policy

Last updated: November 7, 2026

Onry ("Onry", "we", "us") is a mobile app that helps caregivers log and share events about the people they care for ("care recipients"). This policy explains what information Onry collects, how it's used, who can see it, and the choices you have.

Information we collect

Onry collects the information you and your care team enter into the app, including:

  • Account information — your email address and password, used to create and sign in to your account, the name you give yourself in the app and, if you add one, your profile photo. Passwords are stored only in hashed form.
  • Care recipient information — the name and, if you choose to add them, the date of birth and photo of each care recipient you set up. Photos are cropped, shrunk and re-encoded on your phone before they're uploaded, which removes the data a camera stores in a photo, such as where it was taken.
  • Care team information — who is on each care recipient's care team and whether they are an admin or a supporter, who owns each care team, the invite links admins create (with an optional note about who each link is for), and requests to join a care team.
  • Event data — the care events you and your care team log (for example, notes, categories and times), who logged each one, and the list of event types for each care recipient. This includes events the app records for you, such as how you responded to a reminder notification (for example, whether it was acted on, snoozed, skipped or missed).
  • Event history — if an event is changed or removed, we keep a copy of the earlier version, so that a care recipient's record can't be lost or quietly rewritten.
  • Places — the named places a care recipient spends time at (for example Home or School), each with a time zone, and which place the care recipient was at, and when. A place is stored only as the name your care team gives it, not as an address or a position on a map.
  • Shifts — if an admin turns shifts on for a care recipient: the kinds of shift, the roster, when each person went on and off duty, the answers to prompts and to-dos, and the notes admins add when they check a shift.
  • Walks — if you use place and motion sensing (see Location and motion): the start and end time, steps and distance of walks with the care recipient.

We don't collect analytics, advertising identifiers, or device tracking data, and your phone's location never reaches our servers.

Location and motion

On iPhone, Onry can use your phone's location and motion to notice where the care recipient is, and when you go out or come back, so you don't have to tap to record it. This is optional, and your location stays on your phone: our servers never receive coordinates, only the name of one of the care recipient's places, as if you had picked it yourself.

Who turns it on

  • An admin turns place sensing on for each care recipient. While it's off, no phone on the care team senses anything for that care recipient.
  • You choose when your own phone is used: Never, While I'm clocked in, During my rostered shifts or Always. This is set on your phone, and nobody else can change it.
  • iOS asks your permission first: location "While Using" to suggest places, "Always" for the modes that work while Onry is closed, and Motion & Fitness. You can change these in iOS Settings at any time.

What stays on your phone

  • A map of the care recipient's places, learned from where your phone was when you logged a place or tapped I'm here now. It's kept in your phone's secure storage, isn't included in iCloud backups or moved to a new phone, and is deleted when you sign out. You can see which places your phone has learned, and forget them, in the app.
  • Every location reading. iOS tells Onry when your phone arrives at or leaves one of the care recipient's places (at most 20 of them), and Onry uses your current location to suggest which place you're at when you log something.
  • Your motion history and step counts, which iOS records. Onry reads them to tell whether you're carrying your phone, and to find walks.

What your phone sends

Your phone's movements only count while you're with the care recipient: while you're on a shift with them, or after you've said they're with you. At other times, such as on your commute or while someone else is looking after them, Onry records nothing about where your phone goes. While they count, your phone can log:

  • which place the care recipient has arrived at, or that they're on the move
  • that you've gone out, or come back, if you're one of the family members looking after the care recipient at home
  • a walk with the care recipient, with its steps and distance (these are your steps, standing in for theirs)

Anything your phone logs is shown as coming from your phone (for example "Alex's phone left Home at 08:30"), and you're asked to confirm it. Tapping Wrong undoes it. When Onry can't tell that you're carrying your phone, it asks you instead of logging.

Location and motion features aren't available on Android.

Information stored on your device

Onry keeps your sign-in session and the map of the care recipient's places in your device's secure storage. Your reminder settings, when your phone may be used for location and motion, and the care recipient you last selected are stored on your device only.

Notifications

Many reminders are scheduled by your own phone. Onry also sends notifications from our servers, for example when someone asks to join a care team or a prompt needs another carer. To reach your phone, we keep the notification address Apple gives it. We remove it when you sign out on that phone or turn these notifications off in Settings, and when your account is deleted. Notifications pass through Expo and Apple, so their text is kept plain: who did what kind of thing, never the details of a care event.

How we use your information

Information you provide is used only to operate the app for you and your care team:

  • To create your account and authenticate you.
  • To store and display the events and care team data you and your care team enter.
  • To share event data with the people an admin has added to a care recipient's care team.
  • To limit how quickly certain actions can be repeated (such as creating invite links or logging events), which protects your data if an account is misused.
  • To respond to you when you contact us.

We do not sell your information, and we do not use it for advertising.

Who can see your data

Data about a care recipient is visible only to the members of that care recipient's care team. Access is enforced at the database level, so a caregiver can only see care recipients whose team they have joined.

  • Admins can see the care recipient's full event history, including earlier versions of changed or removed events.
  • Supporters can see events logged in the last 7 days, plus any events they logged themselves.
  • Places and walks are events, so they're visible to the care team in the same way.
  • Shifts — admins can see everyone's shifts and time on duty. Supporters can see their own, and the names of the people on duty with the care recipient now, but not anyone else's kind of shift or hours.
  • Your name and profile photo are visible to the other members of every care team you're on.
  • Joining a care team — invite links grant nothing by themselves. When you use one, the care recipient's admins see your name and email address so they can decide whether to approve you. You can't see anything about the care recipient until you're approved. Invite links work once and expire after 7 days, and we store only a fingerprint of each link, never the link itself.

If you leave a care team, or an admin removes you, you lose access to that care recipient. The events you logged stay on the care recipient's record for the rest of the team.

Service providers

We use a small number of service providers to run Onry:

  • Supabase hosts our database and photos, and handles authentication.
  • Expo delivers our notifications to your phone through Apple. It receives each notification's text and your phone's notification address.
  • Cloudflare R2 stores our nightly database backups. Backups are encrypted before they leave our systems, and Cloudflare cannot read them.
  • Formspree receives messages you send through our contact form.

We don't share your data with anyone else.

Deleting your data

Deleting your account

You can delete your account in the app, from Settings → Delete Account. If you're the owner of any care teams, you first choose for each one whether to hand ownership to another admin or to delete that care recipient's record along with your account.

Your account is deleted 30 days after you ask. Straight away, you lose access to your care teams, your teammates see you as leaving the team, your open invite links stop working and your pending join requests are withdrawn. During those 30 days you can sign in and restore your account. After 30 days your name, profile photo, settings, email address and password are deleted permanently. The events you logged are kept, because they are part of each care recipient's record. They are shown with a label such as "Former carer 1" instead of your name, which an admin of that care recipient can rename.

Deleting a care recipient's record

The owner of a care team can delete the care recipient's record in the app. It disappears for everyone on the care team straight away, and the owner can restore it for 30 days. After 30 days the care recipient's record and everything recorded about them (their photo, events, event history, event types, places, shifts and roster, prompts, care team, invite links and join requests) are deleted permanently.

Backups and retention

We keep your data for as long as your account or the care recipient's record is active, and delete it as described above. Encrypted backups are kept so we can recover from a failure: daily backups for 30 days, weekly backups for 120 days, and monthly backups for one year. Deleted data can remain in these backups until they expire. Backups are only used to restore the service after a failure. If we ever restore one, we'll tell you, because a deletion you asked for after the backup was made may need to be asked for again.

If you can't use the in-app options, or want something else deleted, contact us.

Security

Data is encrypted in transit between the app and our servers. Access to every care recipient's data is checked by the database itself for each request, not just by the app. You can sign out of every device at once from Settings if you lose a phone or signed in on one that isn't yours. Signing out on a phone also deletes its map of the care recipient's places and stops it noticing when it arrives at or leaves them.

Children's privacy

Onry is intended for use by caregivers (adults). A care recipient may be a child or other dependent, but their information is entered and controlled by their caregivers, not by the care recipient directly.
